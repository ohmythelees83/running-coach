## 1. The Course

- **Elevation:** ~1,900ft (560m) total gain over 100 miles — one of the
  flattest 100-milers in the UK. The only real climb is a short trail
  section between Whitchurch and Goring; otherwise gains come from short
  bridge-crossing stairs/ramps (rarely more than 20-30 steps). ([Centurion course notes](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2019/section/course%20notes), [GB Ultra Runner](https://www.gbultrarunner.co.uk/ultra-marathons/100-mile/centurion-thames-path-100))
- **Surface:** 75% trail, the rest paved towpath/paths — "groomed paths and
  paved pathway" for the most part. ([Centurion course notes](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2019/section/course%20notes))
- **The catch in the flat profile:** the first ~40 miles are mostly good
  surface, but as tarmac/hardpack gives way to meadows and single-track
  later on, especially in wet conditions, it can turn to "sticky mud with
  tree roots... every step sees your foot slip to the side," particularly
  across the last ~25 miles. ([unwilling veteran, 2023 report](https://unwilling-veteran.sport.blog/2023/05/15/race-report-2023-thames-path-100/), [search summary](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2023))
- **Technical difficulty:** minimal in the classic sense (no scrambling,
  no real climbs) — but multiple experienced 100-mile finishers rate this
  course as harder than its profile suggests, precisely *because* it's
  flat: "there is nothing to naturally break up your pacing or gait," and
  one runner with 7-8 prior hundred-mile finishes called it "by far his
  hardest." ([Kesgrave Kruisers](https://kesgravekruisers.uk/blog/centurion-thames-path-100/))
- **Altitude:** negligible — the entire course sits in the Thames Valley,
  effectively at or near sea level throughout. No altitude consideration.

## 2. Conditions

- **Typical May weather, Thames Valley/Oxfordshire:** average high ~16°C
  (60°F), average low ~7°C (44°F), ~62mm of rain spread over ~7 days in the
  month. Mild, but real rain probability, not a dry-season race. ([WeatherAPI Bicester history](https://www.weatherapi.com/history/may/q/bicester-2781038), [climate-data.org Oxford](https://en.climate-data.org/europe/united-kingdom/england/oxford-22/))
- **What this means practically:** the wet/mud risk in the last 25 miles
  described above is not a rare edge case — May in this region regularly
  produces rain, and the 2023 finisher report above describes exactly this
  scenario undoing an otherwise well-paced race. Plan for both a dry-course
  and a wet-course version of race day.
- **Heat as a separate risk:** even without extreme heat, multiple finisher
  reports describe the sun/exposure on the open towpath "picking people
  off" from around mile 12 onward on a warmer day — the course offers very
  little shade. ([RunningandStuff pacing](http://www.runningandstuff.com/blog/thames-path-100-pacing))

## 3. What Finishers Warn About

- **Going out too fast.** The flat course feels deceptively fast and
  comfortable early — multiple reports describe a "fast and furious" start
  that isn't sustainable, with the heat/effort catching people from ~mile
  12. One runner's plan (9-minute miles from the gun, "slow down if it
  becomes challenging") illustrates the trap: the pace *feels* sustainable
  precisely because nothing forces you to slow down until damage is
  already done. ([RunningandStuff pacing](http://www.runningandstuff.com/blog/thames-path-100-pacing))
- **The monotony itself is the difficulty.** "A flat race is just as
  difficult as a hilly race because you're using the same muscles in the
  same way all the time." Several strong, experienced runners rate this
  course harder than technical, hilly 100-milers specifically because nothing
  varies the load. ([unwilling veteran, 2023](https://unwilling-veteran.sport.blog/2023/05/15/race-report-2023-thames-path-100/), [Kesgrave Kruisers](https://kesgravekruisers.uk/blog/centurion-thames-path-100/))
- **Blisters, specifically from this course's conditions.** With 100,000+
  steps, camber on the towpath, and shoes that pick up morning dew or wet
  grass early, blister risk is a recurring theme — one report describes
  blisters and sores building progressively through the day from exactly
  this combination, "no blister sock can entirely protect your feet." ([Steel City Striders report](https://www.steelcitystriders.co.uk/2019/thames-path-challenge-ultra-100km-2019-report-by-russell-stevenson/))
- **Mental fade is real despite the lack of technical terrain.** One pacer
  reported their "brain was in bed" for the first 12 miles of their pacing
  leg, with several points of genuinely considering a drop — the mental
  battle doesn't disappear just because the terrain is easy underfoot.
- **Overuse injury building through the race itself.** The 2023 report
  above describes an overuse issue that "became increasingly problematic as
  the race went on" compounding the mud/wet-conditions problem — directly
  relevant given your own knee history.
- **Attrition is genuinely high.** At least one year saw a 42% DNF rate,
  and another saw roughly 40% (132 of 330 starters) not finish, largely
  attributed to bad weather underfoot. This is not a race where finishing
  is a formality once you're fit enough to start. ([search result](https://www.londonheathside.org.uk/reports/centurion-thames-path-100-mile-mk-half-marathon-best-of-the-rest))

## 4. Gear and Logistics

- **Shoes:** trail shoes are advised generally, but road shoes with good
  grip are "perfectly adequate" if the course is dry — worth planning for
  both, and a mid-race shoe change (via drop bag at Henley or Goring) is a
  realistic option if the back-half mud materializes. ([search summary, GB Ultra Runner](https://www.gbultrarunner.co.uk/ultra-marathons/100-mile/centurion-thames-path-100))
- **Cupless race:** you must carry your own cup at aid stations — soft
  cups are sold via the Centurion store. ([Centurion FAQs](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2025/section/faqs))
- **Mandatory kit (confirmed items, from recent years' infopacks — full
  2027 list not yet published, reconfirm nearer the time):** fully charged
  mobile phone with Race Medic/Director numbers saved, water
  bottle/bladder capacity of at least 1 litre, waterproof jacket (Gore-Tex
  or equivalent, 10,000mm+ with taped seams), a long-sleeved base
  layer/fleece carried separately in a dry bag for emergency use only, a
  cup, and a physical compass (not electronic). A missing item at any kit
  check costs a 1-hour time penalty. Given the race runs into darkness, a
  head torch (plus almost certainly a spare/backup light) is a near-certain
  addition to this list — **confirm exact spec in the 2027 infopack.** ([Centurion infopack](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2026/section/infopack))
- **Drop bags:** one per aid station used (at minimum Henley and Goring),
  max ~45×25×25cm — no paper bags or shoeboxes, as they can get wet and
  tear. Collect from the finish between 1000-1500 Sunday or they're
  disposed of. ([Centurion FAQs](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2025/section/faqs))
- **Pacers:** permitted from the Henley aid station (mile 51) onward, on
  foot only, one pacer with you at a time (though you can rotate pacers
  across the last 49 miles). ([Centurion crew instructions](https://centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2022/section/crew%20instructions))
- **Crew access:** restricted to specific listed points (parking/noise
  limits in sensitive riverside areas) — crew cannot meet you anywhere
  they choose. Plan crew logistics around the official crew point list
  once published for 2027.
- **Night running:** the race explicitly advises recceing the sections
  you'll cover in darkness and knowing road crossings in advance — this
  is guidance worth taking literally given your current lack of any
  long-run experience, let alone night-running experience, since the
  layoff. ([Centurion infopack](https://www.centurionrunning.com/races/thames-path-100-2026/section/infopack))
- **Travel:** Richmond (start) and Oxford (finish) are both very
  accessible from Basingstoke — roughly 45-60 minutes to Richmond, ~40
  minutes to Oxford by road. Point-to-point logistics (getting back to
  your car/Richmond afterward) need planning, but this isn't a
  remote-access race. Packet pickup timing not yet confirmed for 2027 —
  confirm once the infopack is published.

## 7. What This Means I Need to Train

Tied directly to the gaps above, roughly in priority order:

1. **Protect the start line above everything else.** Symptom-first
   monitoring stays non-negotiable (already in your Coaching Philosophy) —
   this is the single highest-leverage thing between now and May 2027,
   more than any specific session.
2. **Build continuous running duration well beyond the current ~1-hour
   cap**, progressively, toward genuinely long continuous efforts — this
   course's real demand is repetitive muscular load over many hours, not
   technical terrain, so durability-of-motion matters more than
   vert-specific strength here.
3. **Rehearse race pacing deliberately on long runs** — specifically
   practicing restraint in the first hour even when effort feels easy,
   since that's the exact trap this course sets.
4. **Rehearse race-day fueling in training**, including eating solid aid
   station food on the move and practicing with a carried cup, building
   toward tested carb-per-hour targets rather than guessing on the day.
5. **Rebuild weekly volume toward your historical capacity**, but on the
   ~10% guideline over many months — not compressed to fit an artificial
   timeline.
6. **Build a tested blister-prevention strategy** (socks, lubricant, wet-
   shoe contingency) specific to this course's step count and camber,
   proven in training before race day.
7. **Practice night running logistics** — headtorch, backup light,
   navigation of unfamiliar sections in darkness — well before race week.
8. **Keep using back-to-back long weekends deliberately** (already in your
   plan) as the training tool that most directly targets the
   same-muscle-repetition durability finishers flag as this course's real
   test.
