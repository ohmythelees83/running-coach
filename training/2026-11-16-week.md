## The compromise, stated plainly

The 9–15 Nov data doesn't actually point at distance as the problem —
Monday's 4.1mi was the cleanest, most controlled session of that whole
week. What compounded was pace, hills, and no rest day before the
longest session, all three stacked onto one run (Sunday). So this week
advances distance, which is what was asked for, while holding firm on
the three variables that actually drifted:

- **Distance moves** — ~19mi this week vs. 18mi actual last week, via
  reintroducing the back-to-back rather than pushing the midweek sessions
  longer than Monday 9 Nov already proved comfortable. Saturday carries
  most of the increase (6mi); Sunday trimmed slightly (4mi) to match.
- **Pace does not move** — every session genuinely easy/conversational.
  If HR passes ~130 before halfway through Tuesday's or Thursday's run,
  that's the cue to ease off, not push through — the exact thing that let
  Saturday's and Sunday's sessions drift into Z3 early.
- **Terrain does not move** — flat routes only this week. Hills come
  back once pacing is proven solid on flat ground again.
- **Rest days do not move** — Monday and Friday stay genuinely rest, no
  repeat of this week's spontaneous Monday/Sunday runs bypassing them.
